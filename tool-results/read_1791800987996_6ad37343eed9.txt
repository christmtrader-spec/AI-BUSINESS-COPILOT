    20→    20→## 1. Scope and method
    21→    21→
    22→    22→One row per data category actually stored or processed by this product today. Every row was
    23→    23→written against the Prisma schema and the route audit — categories that do not exist (e.g. contact
    24→    24→leads) are listed as **NOT IMPLEMENTED** rather than omitted, so the inventory cannot imply a
    25→    25→collection that does not happen. What each row means:
    26→    26→
    27→    27→- **Origin** — `U:` = values provided by a user; `S:` = values generated by the system.
    28→    28→- **Third-party processed** — only seams that actually exist in code. The single AI seam is
    29→    29→  `src/lib/ai/provider.ts` (z-ai-web-dev-sdk adapter, server-only). Payments use a provider
    30→    30→  abstraction with a mock provider and a Zarinpal sandbox adapter; production is NOT active and no
    31→    31→  merchant credentials exist. Email is a provider-agnostic boundary (`src/lib/email/index.ts`) with
    32→    32→  a dev console transport only — production email is **CURRENTLY NOT EXPLICITLY CONFIGURED**.
    33→    33→  There is no third-party analytics of any kind.
    34→    34→- **Exported?** — whether the category leaves the system through the implemented export paths
    35→    35→  (business JSON backup `GET /api/v1/businesses/[id]/export`; CSV reports under the `CSV_EXPORT`
    36→    36→  entitlement). Both are listed exactly as implemented; see `docs/legal/user-data-rights.md` §5.
    37→    37→- **Deleted? / Retention status** — what happens on account deletion and business deletion, and
    38→    38→  whether an independent retention policy exists. Full per-category detail:
    39→    39→  `docs/legal/data-retention-inventory.md`; operation-by-operation matrix:
    40→    40→  `docs/legal/data-deletion-matrix.md`.

    42→    42→## 2. Classification model
    43→    43→
    44→    44→Four tiers. Assignment is an engineering judgment for handling decisions — it is not a legal
    45→    45→designation, and it does not map one-to-one onto any statute's definitions.
    46→    46→
    47→    47→| Tier | Definition | Reasoning examples in this product |
    48→    48→|---|---|---|
    49→    49→| **PUBLIC** | Safe to expose without authentication; no harm from disclosure | Public pricing (`/pricing`, `GET /api/public/pricing` — plan names and prices with honest availability states); the public legal pages themselves; `robots.txt` |
    50→    50→| **INTERNAL** | Aggregated, derived, or operational data; harmful mainly in aggregate or to the operator's interests | Plan configuration and entitlements; AI usage metadata (token counts, no content); commercial funnel events; the AI cost ledgers and provider rate registry (operator-only, never tenant-visible); webhook replay ledger |
    51→    51→| **CONFIDENTIAL** | Business records or personal data; disclosure harms a tenant or a natural person | Business financial records (sales, expenses, payments); customer/supplier contact details; copilot transcripts; audit trail (incl. IP, user agent); member identities; legal acceptance records |
    52→    52→| **HIGHLY_SENSITIVE** | Credentials, secrets, or material that directly enables account or system compromise | Password hashes (bcrypt cost 12); session tokens (`NEXTAUTH_SECRET`-signed cookies); password-reset token hashes; AI provider credentials behind the server-only provider seam |
    53→    53→
    54→    54→Note: `docs/security/data-classification.md` (Prompt 17) labels the CONFIDENTIAL tier
    55→    55→"SENSITIVE" with the same intent; this inventory uses the four names above and both documents
    56→    56→agree on membership. `next.config.ts` defines **no global CSP** — confidentiality of the tiers
    57→    57→above rests on the tenant pipeline, RBAC, and the other controls listed in that document, and the
    58→    58→CSP gap is tracked there.

   166→   166→### 4.9 Notifications (Prompt 32)
   167→   167→
   168→   168→Three user-owned notification models record derived activity communication
   169→   169→(`Notification`, `NotificationDelivery`, `NotificationPreference`; `userId` →
   170→   170→`User`, `onDelete: Cascade`, plus an optional `businessId` → `Business` cascade
   171→   171→on `Notification`). Notifications are a communication layer derived from domain
   172→   172→events — never an authority layer. All stored values are allowlisted catalog
   173→   173→keys (type/category/priority/severity) plus a per-type schema-validated data
   174→   174→object of safe primitives; a defense-in-depth sanitizer rejects secret-shaped
   175→   175→keys (password/token/secret/otp/mfa/recoveryCode/session/reset/credential/
   176→   176→merchant …) at any depth, AI prompt/response and provider-cost keys, IP/UA
   177→   177→keys, and oversized payloads. Localized text is never persisted (i18n keys +
   178→   178→rendered at read time). Security reference:
   179→   179→`docs/security/notification-security.md`; catalog and data sensitivity per
   180→   180→type: `docs/product/notification-matrix.md`.

   233→   233→## 5. Business data vs personal data
   234→   234→
   235→   235→The tenant/business split is the backbone of this inventory, and it is deliberately conservative:
   236→   236→
   237→   237→1. **Most rows in §4.2 are business data, not personal data.** Sales, expenses, products, and
   238→   238→   payment records exist to run a business and are owned by the business context, not the
   239→   239→   individual account. They are deleted through **business deletion**, and a member's account
   240→   240→   deletion does not remove them (`docs/legal/data-deletion-matrix.md` §4 — the owner-block makes
   241→   241→   this explicit: a user cannot delete business-owned data through account deletion).
   242→   242→2. **Business data can simultaneously be personal data.** `Customer`/`Supplier` contact fields
   243→   243→   (name, email, phone, address) may identify natural persons — sole traders, contact people at
   244→   244→   customer companies. Dual characterization is flagged, not resolved: final treatment is
   245→   245→   UNKNOWN-REVIEW REQUIRED pending legal review (the content registry itself carries the
   246→   246→   `legalReviewRequired` marker).
   247→   247→3. **The same person appears in several roles with different deletion fates:** as an account owner
   248→   248→   (User row — deleted with account), as a business member (membership row — deleted with account;
   249→   249→   the business survives), as an audit actor (link removed via SetNull, history row stays), and as
   250→   250→   the author of business records (business records stay; they belong to the business).
   251→   251→4. **Platform-level data is neither business nor tenant data**: commercial events, AI cost
   252→   252→   records, webhook ledger, and platform-level audit rows (businessId null, e.g.
   253→   253→   `auth.account_deleted`) persist across both deletion operations.
